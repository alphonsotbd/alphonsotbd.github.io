Concept brief · Site safety intelligence · 2026

SiteGuard

Wearable fall and near-miss detection for construction workers

ESP32-C6BLEPythonSQLite SupabaseStreamlitGrafana
Live operational dashboard · Streamlit
01 — Project scope / client problem

Safety events can go unseen when nobody is watching.

Construction work often happens outside CCTV coverage and without a dedicated safety officer watching every worker. Falls, slips and near misses may therefore be reported late—or not at all. Our team set out to create a low-cost wearable system that detects incidents in real time and alerts a supervisor in the worker’s language.

02 — My role & solution

Storage, Data Quality & Insight Design

I defined validation rules and storage safeguards, supported the Fall-Risk Index and shaped how trusted events appeared in the dashboard experience. Streamlit gave supervisors an immediate operational view, while Grafana exposed incident trends and location hotspots.

03 — Work process

One continuous pipeline

  1. 01Sense4 NESSO N1 wearables stream IMU data
  2. 02ValidateCheck schema, confidence, event type and duplicates
  3. 03StoreDual-write to local SQLite and cloud Supabase
  4. 04ActDashboards visualise risk; Telegram sends alerts
04 — Outcome & results achieved

A working end-to-end safety system—not a slide-only concept.

Four physical wearables streamed live through ingestion, validation, cleaning, storage, visualisation and automated alerting. Testing also exposed the limits of magnitude-only detection, so the team adopted jerk-based logic and documented a safety-first trade-off.

01 — Project scope / client problem

Which subjects should a new tuition company specialise in?

A tuition company needed a dependable view of school and subject coverage for students aged 7–17 in North and East Singapore. The two official datasets were useful but not analysis-ready: they used different row structures, inconsistent text and school-level labels instead of student ages.

02 — My role & solution

Solo data engineer—from raw tables to decision-ready features.

I designed an auditable MySQL and Pandas workflow. MySQL preserved raw lineage and filtered the target population; Pandas standardised, validated, deduplicated and integrated the exports. I then produced a school-level feature matrix and a hybrid SQL/NoSQL storage proposal.

03 — Work process

Every transformation remained traceable.

01StageImport both official CSVs as text to preserve identifiers.
02FilterDefine an explicit school-level proxy for ages 7–17 and retain North/East records.
03CleanStandardise labels, repair domain issues, handle missing values and remove canonical duplicates.
04IntegrateJoin subjects to schools, confirm completeness and engineer one feature row per school.
Feature matrix and subject coverage evidence
04 — Outcome & results achieved

Clean data revealed both strong anchors and a possible niche.

The integrated dataset had zero unmatched subject records. English, Mathematics, Science and Chinese appeared across all 161 target schools, making them strong core offerings. Computing appeared in 65 schools (40.4%), making it a possible differentiated enrichment niche that should be tested against demand data.

01 — Project scope / client problem

Air quality alone does not tell the whole safety story.

A room may need different action depending on both pollutant level and whether the door is open. The prototype combined environmental sensing with entry-state context so that its warning was easier to interpret.

02 — My role & solution

I designed the sensing, alert and data-flow logic.

I integrated an air quality sensor and an IR reflective door sensor, mapped combined readings to LCD colour states, added automatic buzzer escalation and connected the microcontroller to SQL storage and a web interface.

03 — Work process

Build the state model, then test every transition.

I defined the four safety states first, implemented sensor reads and output controls, tested the door and air combinations, and checked that severe readings automatically triggered the red display and buzzer without relying on a manual reset.

04 — Outcome & results achieved

A complete monitor-to-record prototype.

The final system translated two live inputs into immediate visual and sound alerts, while sending readings through USB to an SQL database for a web view. It demonstrated how simple context can make an IoT warning more actionable.
